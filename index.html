# PRD–001

Hi, I'm Ruthvik.

Author: S.J. Ruthvik Role: Product Manager Status: open to chats about what's next Last edited: today

TL;DR

I'm a PM with an engineering background. Most of what I do is find the slow or manual step in a process and help rebuild the system around it. I'm currently Technical PM at Akteena. The site's written as a PRD because — well, old habits.

01 — Background

A bit about how I got here.

I trained as an Electronics & Instrumentation engineer at RVCE Bengaluru. After college I worked on telematics for EVs at Log9, then moved into competitive and product analysis on the storage portfolio at Dell. That was where the product work really started clicking — and I shifted into PM properly at Rang De in fintech, working on the loan-review and partner-management side of things. These days I'm Technical PM at Akteena, looking after the SDK and firmware roadmap and the compliance/support side.

If there's a thread running through it: I tend to end up on whatever process is slow, manual or breaking in a way no one's quite owning end-to-end.

02 — What I think you might be hiring for

It usually isn't "more features."

Most product orgs I've seen have at least one process that's slower or more manual than anyone's happy with — it just rarely makes it onto the roadmap because shipping new features feels more visible. The PM roles I'm most interested in are the ones where someone wants help with that — the kind of work where a small set of changes can move something real.

If the role is "ship the next thing on the list, please," that's fair, but I'm probably not the best fit.

03 — Why I think I'd be useful

I've done this work a few times.

The Rang De redesign work (§05) is the clearest example. The Dell pipeline work and the Log9 telematics work are smaller flavours of the same idea: map the process, find the constraint, rebuild around it, instrument it so you can tell if it actually moved.

None of that's magic — it's just where I've spent most of my time, and I'm faster at it now than I was three years ago.

04 — How I tend to work

A few things I try to do.

01    Start at the slow step, not the feature.

Most of my better work has come from looking for the actual constraint first. The spec follows the constraint, not the other way around.

02    Build the system, not just the tool.

A loan-review tool is a tool. A loan-review system that's measurably faster and cheaper is what tends to stick. The second is usually the more interesting problem.

03    Instrument before launch, not after.

If we can't tell from a dashboard whether the metric moved, I'm not really sure if we changed anything. So I try to get the measurement in place before the change ships.

06 — Things to flag upfront

What I'm not.

  • I'm not a designer. I can put together decent wireframes, but for anything that ships, you'd want a real designer in the room.
  • I work best when initiatives have a number attached to them. If a role is purely shipping a fixed spec without much "why," I'd probably get restless.
  • I take a bit longer with vague problems than with well-defined ones. I tend to want to understand the system before I commit to a direction.
